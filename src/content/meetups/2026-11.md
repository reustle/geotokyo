We had one of our biggest turnouts yet for the ninth Geo Tokyo Meetup. Once again we were hosted in FabCafe Tokyo's MTRL space, also home to the long-running and impactful [Safecast](https://safecast.org) project.

The Eukarya team came to introduce two free and open projects, [Re:Earth](https://reearth.io/home) and [Navara](https://navara.world/docs/). Re:Earth is a set of map-making platforms, including terrain data APIs, building data APIs, and [Re:Earth Papers](https://reearth.engineering/posts/reearth-papers-en/), a custom artistic map tile API that draws tiles on demand. The platform is free to use with no API keys, which is possible because it is heavily cached and costs are kept very low. Navara is a 3D map rendering engine designed to render accurate maps, while also letting you completely customise the rendering with shaders and direct access to the Three.js scene. The whole system is built in Rust and compiled to WebAssembly to run in the browser.

After that, we had three demos from attendees. Thomas showed his work on [geo embeddings](https://sonora.tech/embeddings) that are updated on a weekly cadence and designed to be orders of magnitude smaller than other embedding systems. Grisha showed a [custom map](https://maps.gszep.com/) he made just for himself, using the Ordnance Survey tile sets he prefers directly and overlaying the information he's interested in. Alastair showed a complete [ski resort simulation](https://snowline.jp) based on real terrain, features and building data, which you can ski through the same way Google Maps lets you drive through Street View.
